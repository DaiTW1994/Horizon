# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a political news item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about international relations, government policy, geopolitical events, or domestic politics with significant consequences.

## Scoring rubric

- **9-10**: Systemic. Major geopolitical shifts, regime changes, large-scale conflicts, or policies that fundamentally alter international relations or a country's political trajectory.
- **7-8**: Important. Significant election results, major policy legislation, diplomatic breakthroughs or breakdowns, or political events with clear consequences for a large population or region.
- **5-6**: Useful. Credible and concrete political developments that help readers understand a policy direction, political trend, or diplomatic relationship, but have limited immediate impact or breadth.
- **3-4**: Low value. Routine political statements, expected policy moves, minor diplomatic meetings, speculative commentary, or reports lacking specific substance or meaningful context.
- **0-2**: Noise. Unsubstantiated rumors, partisan attacks without factual basis, sensational predictions, or content with no reliable political substance.

## Evaluation guidance

Consider the magnitude and breadth of the political event, source quality, timeliness, affected populations or regions, and whether the item provides concrete details with meaningful context. Distinguish reported facts from analysis and speculation. Do not reward a large headline without evidence of actual consequences, and do not treat political popularity as geopolitical significance.

Use three to five specific topic tags (e.g., "diplomacy", "election", "trade-policy", "geopolitics", "human-rights", "conflict", "regulation").
