# Evaluation goal
Evaluate whether a piece of contemporary art writing gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful insight into artistic practice, institutional dynamics, market trends, or cultural policy.

# Scoring rubric
- **9-10: Systemic.** Major institutional overhauls, museum acquisition or deaccessioning scandals, government funding cuts or new cultural legislation, biennial cancellations or boycotts with broad geopolitical resonance, or artist-led movements that fundamentally challenge prevailing structures of production or display.

- **7-8: Important.** High-profile gallery representations or defections, record-breaking auction results that reset market benchmarks, significant repatriation decisions, curatorial appointments with clear directional signals for major institutions, or critical reception that meaningfully shifts discourse around a significant body of work.

- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and concrete studio practice reports, exhibition reviews that engage seriously with form and concept rather than press-release talking points, transparent auction or fair sales data, or documentation of institutional policies that affect a defined artist or curator community.

- **3-4: Low value.** Routine gallery openings, predictable career moves, superficial market roundups without long-term context, fluff interviews, or social-media-driven controversies that lack evidence or lasting consequence.

- **0-2: Noise.** Unsubstantiated attribution rumors, promotional hype disguised as criticism, prices cited without edition size or condition, sensational predictions about "the next big thing," or content with no discernible connection to actual artworks, artists, or institutions.

# Evaluation guidance
Consider the magnitude and breadth of the event, source quality (primary vs. secondary, institutional vs. speculative), timeliness, affected groups (artists, galleries, collectors, museums, policymakers), and whether the item provides concrete figures (sale prices, attendance, budgets) with meaningful baselines (previous sales, prior-year attendance, pledged versus actual funding). Distinguish reported facts from curatorial statements and critical opinions. Do not reward a dramatic price jump without considering the artist's prior market history, and do not treat social-media virality as art-historical or economic importance.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
